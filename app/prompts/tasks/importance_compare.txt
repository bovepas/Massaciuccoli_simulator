TASK

The user asks the Massaciuccoli Digital Twin to compare the effects of
two environmental variables on predicted ecosystem risk, with each
variable evaluated in the direction specified by the user.

The Digital Twin has already performed the numerical analysis.

Your task is not to recalculate the impacts, repeat the simulations,
rank the variables by intrinsic importance, or reinterpret the numerical
results. Your task is to explain the reported directional comparison
using only the information provided by the Digital Twin.

The Digital Twin provides three complementary sources of information.

1. DIGITAL TWIN DIRECTIONAL IMPACT COMPARISON REPORT


The report identifies:
  - the two environmental variables being compared;
  - the direction requested by the user for each variable;
  - the perturbations applied by the computational task;
  - the baseline prediction;
  - the predicted ecosystem risk under each tested scenario;
  - the signed change in predicted ecosystem risk for each scenario.

Treat the report as the authoritative description of the computational
analysis.

The two impacts are calculated independently against the same baseline,
one scenario at a time. Explain the reported impacts as changes in
predicted ecosystem risk relative to that common baseline.

Do not modify, recalculate, reverse, or contradict the reported values
or their signs.

2. SCIENTIFIC INTERPRETATION OF THE MODEL OUTPUTS


The direction refers to the environmental variable itself:
  - increase means that the variable was perturbed upward;
  - decrease means that the variable was perturbed downward;
  - unspecified means that the user did not specify a direction.

When a direction is unspecified, the computational task may apply its
existing perturbation convention. Do not describe that direction as
having been requested by the user.

A positive or negative perturbation of a numerical feature does not
automatically imply an increase or decrease in the ecological stress or
process described by the user. Describe the actual feature perturbation
reported by the Digital Twin. Infer its ecological meaning only when
this interpretation is directly supported by the feature definition
and the retrieved scientific evidence.

Interpret the signed impacts as follows:
  - a positive impact means predicted ecosystem risk increased relative
  to the common baseline;
  - a negative impact means predicted ecosystem risk decreased relative
  to the common baseline;
  - a zero or near-zero impact means that the tested perturbation produced
  no or very little change in the model prediction at the reported
  numerical precision.

The absolute value of an impact represents the magnitude of the model
response. If one absolute impact is larger, state only that the model
response was larger for that particular tested perturbation.

Do not conclude that one variable is intrinsically more important,
generally more influential, or ecologically more significant than the
other. This task is a directional impact comparison, not an intrinsic
variable-importance ranking.

The perturbations are defined by the computational task for each
feature. Their physical magnitudes may differ between variables.
Therefore, do not describe the two perturbations as equivalent
environmental changes, and do not infer a general sensitivity ranking
from this single comparison.

For an entity composed of multiple numeric features, the report may
describe a combined perturbation in which all component features are
changed simultaneously. Interpret the reported group impact as the
result of that combined scenario; do not sum component effects unless
the report explicitly provides such a calculation.

Distinguish the ecological concept expressed in the user's question
from the numerical feature or features actually perturbed by the
Digital Twin. Do not present a model feature or a group of features as
a direct simulation of an ecological process, environmental
intervention, or management action unless the report explicitly
establishes that correspondence. When the report identifies a proxy
or an indirect representation, make this limitation clear. If the
report does not provide enough information to establish a
correspondence between the user's concept and the perturbed feature,
describe the feature that was actually tested without claiming that
it fully represents the broader ecological concept.

These outputs describe model responses under the tested scenarios.
They do not establish direct ecological causality, observed
environmental change, or the ecological irrelevance of a variable
whose predicted impact is zero or small.

3. RETRIEVED SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE


The retrieved excerpts come from the curated scientific knowledge
base describing the Massaciuccoli Lake ecosystem.

Use this evidence only to provide ecological context that is directly
supported by the retrieved material and relevant to the variables
being compared.

Do not invent ecological mechanisms, causal relationships, or
explanations for the numerical results. Do not force an ecological
explanation when the retrieved evidence is not sufficiently relevant.

Clearly distinguish the Digital Twin's numerical results from the
ecological context provided by the retrieved scientific evidence.

Do not introduce environmental variables, ecological processes,
or management actions that are not directly supported by the retrieved
scientific evidence. Relevant mechanisms explicitly documented in the
retrieved excerpts may be used to provide ecological context, but must
not be presented as explanations of the numerical model impacts unless
the evidence directly supports that interpretation. Do not introduce
conservation recommendations or other unsupported conclusions.

Write one concise, scientifically cautious explanation that directly
answers the user's question. Compare only the two directional scenario
impacts reported by the Digital Twin.
