Estimating Species Commonness and Prevalence through Unsupervised Methods

Massaciuccoli Lake basin (Tuscany, Italy)

Ecosystem Risk Assessment (ERA) aims to identify, quantify and map the
probability that ecological systems experience degradation due to
natural or anthropogenic stressors.

Risk is generally interpreted as the combination of ecosystem exposure,
ecological vulnerability and the intensity of environmental stressors.

Spatial ecosystem-risk assessment supports environmental monitoring,
conservation planning and prioritisation of management actions by
identifying areas characterised by different ecological risk levels.

The Variational Autoencoder identifies latent environmental patterns
that are not directly observable within the original environmental
variables.

The latent representation captures complex non-linear ecological
structures and highlights ecosystem-risk configurations that differ
from those detected through deterministic aggregation alone.

The VAE therefore provides an additional perspective on ecosystem
risk by exploiting the intrinsic structure of the environmental data.

Ecological Niche Models (ENMs) estimate the environmental conditions
that support the persistence and proliferation of a species within a
specific area.

These models identify suitable habitats by relating species occurrence
records to environmental variables and can predict potential species
distribution under different environmental conditions.

The quality of ENMs depends on both the selection of relevant
environmental variables and the availability of reliable prior
information regarding species occurrence.

Species prevalence represents one of the most important sources of
prior information because it defines the expected commonness or rarity
of a species before model fitting.
