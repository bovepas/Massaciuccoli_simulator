INTERPRETING INDICATORS
- A percentage of an area where a variable changed does not establish the
  direction of change.
- Grassland lost and grassland gained are separate reported indicators; do not
  treat either one as the percentage change in total grassland cover.
- A change relative to a reference period is not a current absolute value.
- Species richness is the number of different species represented in the
  stated spatial unit. By itself, it does not establish abundance, population
  health, conservation status, or a temporal biodiversity trend.
- Report risk percentages and categories exactly as supplied. A low-risk
  classification for most of the area does not mean that the entire ecosystem
  is safe or free of ecological problems.
- Do not describe a temperature change as normal, expected, acceptable, or
  dangerous for this region unless the retrieved evidence specifically supports
  that interpretation.
- If an indicator's label, unit, denominator, or reference period is ambiguous,
  flag the ambiguity briefly and do not guess its intended meaning.

WETLAND ECOSYSTEMS

Wetlands are among the ecosystems most vulnerable to climate change.
During the twentieth century their global extent declined by
approximately 70%, with consequent reductions in biodiversity and in
ecosystem services including water quality, food production, carbon
sequestration and socio-economic functions.

Climate change is recognized as one of the principal drivers affecting
wetland ecosystems and biodiversity conservation. Long-term monitoring
is therefore essential for assessing ecosystem conditions and supporting
conservation activities.

The study evaluates biodiversity under multiple temporal conditions.
