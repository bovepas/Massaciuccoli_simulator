DOCUMENT: Venturi et al. (2021) – Methane and carbon dioxide emissions in Massaciuccoli and Porta Lakes
SOURCE: Venturi, S., Tassi, F., Bicocchi, G., et al. (2021). “Exploring Methane Emission Drivers in Wetlands: The Cases of Massaciuccoli and Porta Lakes (Northern Tuscany, Italy).” Applied Sciences, 11, 12156. https://doi.org/10.3390/app112412156
CASE STUDY: Massaciuccoli Lake system (Tuscany, Italy); comparative case study: Porta Lake
DOMAIN: Wetland biogeochemistry; dissolved gases; methane (CH4); carbon dioxide (CO2); greenhouse-gas diffusive fluxes; vegetation and hydrodynamic controls
PURPOSE: Provide source-grounded evidence for retrieval questions about the chemical and physical controls on dissolved CH4 and CO2, their diffusive emissions, spatial variability, reed-bed effects, and limitations of the observations.

============================================================
STUDY DESIGN, SAMPLING AND METHODS
============================================================

The paper compares two coastal wetland systems in northern Tuscany: Massaciuccoli Lake and Porta Lake. Results from the two systems must be kept distinct because their environmental settings, sampling designs and measured fluxes differ.

Massaciuccoli Lake was investigated during a single sampling campaign in November 2020. Sampling covered different environments within the lake system, including open-water locations, sites near the shore, tributary and outlet channels, and the Cava Sisa quarry lake. Vertical profiles were collected at two locations in the main lake (OMW1 and OMW2) and at one location in Cava Sisa (OSW1).

Porta Lake was sampled in August and December 2020, allowing a summer–winter comparison. Seasonal findings from Porta must not be presented as seasonal observations for Massaciuccoli.

The investigation included physicochemical parameters, dissolved gases and stable carbon isotopes. Dissolved gas composition included CO2, N2, Ar, CH4 and O2. Carbon isotope compositions of dissolved CO2 and CH4 were reported as δ13C-CO2 and δ13C-CH4, respectively, in per mil relative to V-PDB.

Diffusive gas fluxes across the water–air interface were calculated using the Thin Boundary Layer (TBL) model. These are model-derived estimates based on measured dissolved-gas concentrations and gas-exchange parameterisation; they are not direct chamber measurements and do not represent a complete measurement of all emission pathways.

============================================================
CHEMICAL CHARACTERISTICS OF THE MASSACIUCCOLI LAKE SYSTEM
============================================================

The chemical composition of waters in the Massaciuccoli Lake system is generally dominated by Na+ and Cl−. The authors identify several factors shaping water chemistry:
- variability in the chemical composition of feeding waters;
- direct mixing with seawater;
- anthropogenic inputs from urban, agricultural and industrial areas;
- biogeochemical processes associated with eutrophication;
- evaporation.

The Na+/Cl− mass ratios reported for the system range from 0.50 to 0.69, consistent with a significant seawater influence. The average Cl−/Br− mass ratio is 308, close to the seawater value reported by the authors (292). Higher Cl−/Br− ratios (≥384) occur in some samples, particularly from Cava Sisa and in one tributary-channel sample. The authors discuss anthropogenic inputs and/or bromide assimilation by green plants and phytoplankton as possible explanations; these are interpretations, not uniquely demonstrated sources.

Water stable-isotope compositions indicate substantial evaporative fractionation in most sampled waters. The Case Rosse spring (SPW) and most tributary-channel samples (TCW) are less affected by this signal, which the authors relate to lower exposure to solar radiation.

Nitrate concentrations reach 31 mg/L in tributary-channel sample TCW5 and 17 mg/L in outlet-channel sample ECW8. These are sample-specific values from the November 2020 campaign, not annual averages or basin-wide estimates.

============================================================
VERTICAL PROFILES: MAIN LAKE AND CAVA SISA
============================================================

In the two main-lake vertical profiles, dissolved CO2 and CH4 increase with depth, while O2 decreases. The changes are relatively moderate compared with those observed in Cava Sisa.

OMW1:
- CO2 increases from 0.02 mmol/L at the surface to 0.08 mmol/L in bottom water.
- CH4 increases from 0.0035 mmol/L at the surface to 0.0088 mmol/L at 3 m.

OMW2:
- CO2 increases from 0.02 mmol/L at the surface to 0.10 mmol/L in bottom water.
- CH4 increases from 0.0031 mmol/L at the surface to 0.0078 mmol/L at 2.5 m.

At Cava Sisa (OSW1), the vertical gradients are much stronger:
- CO2 increases from 0.07 mmol/L at the surface to 0.23 mmol/L at 16 m.
- CH4 increases from 0.0087 mmol/L at the surface to 0.56 mmol/L at 16 m.
- O2 decreases with depth; hypoxic conditions occur below 2 m and no dissolved O2 is detected at depths of 10 m and greater.
- CH4 becomes the second most abundant dissolved gas at depths of 8 m and greater.

The authors describe a chemocline at approximately 10 m depth, separating anoxic, ammonium-rich deep waters from shallower waters. They interpret the vertical patterns as evidence of decomposition and mineralisation of organic matter in deep waters and sediments, with methanogenesis contributing to increasing dissolved CH4 and enrichment in the lighter carbon isotope at depth.

Cava Sisa is a deep, stratified quarry lake. Its extreme deep-water concentrations must not be generalised to the shallow main lake or to the entire Massaciuccoli basin.

============================================================
DIFFUSIVE CH4 AND CO2 FLUXES AT MASSACIUCCOLI
============================================================

The CH4 diffusive fluxes calculated for Massaciuccoli range from 0.020 to 1.61 g CH4 m-2 day-1. The highest value, 1.61 g CH4 m-2 day-1, was measured at site LSW7, located within an area largely covered by reed beds. The paper reports this value as particularly high compared with the other Massaciuccoli sampling sites.

The CO2 diffusive fluxes range from negative values to 43.9 g CO2 m-2 day-1. Negative values indicate net CO2 uptake from the atmosphere under the sign convention used in the paper. The highest CO2 flux, 43.9 g CO2 m-2 day-1, was also recorded at LSW7.

The resulting CO2-equivalent diffusive fluxes (CO2eq) range from -2.33 to 89 g CO2eq m-2 day-1, with the maximum at LSW7.

The authors report that the LSW sites, located near the lake shore, generally show the highest CH4 and CO2 fluxes. The exceptionally high LSW7 values demonstrate strong spatial heterogeneity within the lake system. They do not establish a representative mean emission rate for the whole lake.

============================================================
REED BEDS, ORGANIC MATTER AND METHANE PRODUCTION
============================================================

The authors identify reed beds as important local controls on CH4 emissions through two potentially complementary mechanisms:

1. Reed biomass and plant-derived detritus provide labile organic matter that can support microbial methanogenesis in anoxic sediments.
2. Dense reed beds shelter water surfaces from wind, hinder mixing and oxygenation, and can favour stagnant or hypoxic conditions. Reduced oxygen availability may limit aerobic methane oxidation during transport towards the water–air interface.

The δ13C-CH4 values measured in Massaciuccoli, around -65‰ in the interpretation discussed by the authors, are considered consistent with a substantial contribution from acetoclastic methanogenesis and the availability of readily degradable organic substrates.

The highest CH4 flux at LSW7 occurred in a site largely covered by reed beds. The authors interpret this as evidence of the dual role of reed beds: providing organic substrates for CH4 production and creating local hydrodynamic conditions that can favour CH4 preservation and release.

The paper also discusses reed die-back syndrome (RDBS) and previous research suggesting that die-back areas can show enhanced sediment methanogenesis. However, the Massaciuccoli sampling campaign does not constitute a controlled experiment isolating the causal effect of reed die-back on emissions. Do not state that RDBS was demonstrated to increase Massaciuccoli CH4 fluxes by this study.

============================================================
RELATION BETWEEN CH4 AND CO2 FLUXES
============================================================

At Massaciuccoli, high CH4 diffusive fluxes are generally associated with high CO2 fluxes. The authors interpret this relationship as evidence of a common origin linked to organic-matter degradation.

The relationship is not uniform across all sites: photosynthetic activity can produce negative CO2 fluxes, indicating uptake from the atmosphere, while CH4 emissions may still occur. Thus, CO2 uptake at a sampling point does not imply an absence of CH4 emission.

Massaciuccoli shows lower CO2-equivalent fluxes than Porta Lake overall, but includes local emission hotspots, especially LSW7.

============================================================
PORTA LAKE: COMPARATIVE RESULTS ONLY
============================================================

Porta Lake is a separate comparative wetland and its numerical results must not be attributed to Massaciuccoli.

At Porta, CH4 diffusive fluxes ranged from 1.24 to 4.97 g CH4 m-2 day-1 in summer and from 0.397 to 2.65 g CH4 m-2 day-1 in winter. The authors discuss seasonal temperature effects on microbial activity, organic-matter availability, reed-bed conditions and water-column mixing.

The paper reports exceptionally high CH4 fluxes at Porta, in some cases exceeding values reported for many lakes and reservoirs. These results provide a comparison of contrasting wetland conditions, not a calibration range for Massaciuccoli.

============================================================
INTERPRETATION AND IMPLICATIONS
============================================================

The study concludes that Massaciuccoli and Porta are net sources of CH4 and CO2 overall, while local CO2 uptake can occur at individual Massaciuccoli sampling sites. The maximum CO2-equivalent flux reported for the two-lake study is 183 g CO2eq m-2 day-1; this maximum must not be assigned to Massaciuccoli because the paper reports it in the comparison of the two systems and the highest Massaciuccoli sample-level CO2eq value in Table 4 is 89 g m-2 day-1 at LSW7.

The magnitude of CH4 diffusive emissions depends on the balance between CH4 production and consumption. Methane oxidation is mainly associated with aerobic methanotrophy and is influenced by dissolved O2. Oxygen conditions, in turn, depend on water mixing and stratification, which the authors relate to wind, lake morphology and vegetation.

Methane production is influenced by seasonal temperature variations and by the quality and quantity of available organic matter. Reed beds can provide shelter from wind and labile organic matter, and sites close to reed beds can have CH4 fluxes up to two orders of magnitude higher than open-water sites in the Massaciuccoli dataset.

The authors emphasise that wetland CH4 emissions are strongly site-dependent and vary in both space and time. Sampling design, timing and site selection can therefore substantially affect estimates of total emissions. They recommend increasing the spatial and temporal resolution of CH4 flux measurements and combining traditional diffusive-flux measurements with low-cost CH4 sensors in flux chambers.

============================================================
LIMITATIONS AND APPROPRIATE USE IN THE MASSACIUCCOLI DIGITAL TWIN
============================================================

Key limitations:
- Massaciuccoli was sampled during one campaign only (November 2020); this paper does not provide a seasonal time series for Massaciuccoli.
- Sampling locations do not provide exhaustive spatial coverage of the lake system.
- Diffusive fluxes were calculated using the TBL model and are not direct chamber measurements.
- The study does not quantify all possible emission pathways, including ebullition and plant-mediated transport, as separate basin-wide flux components.
- The very high LSW7 value is a local hotspot, not a representative lake-wide average.
- Cava Sisa is a deep, stratified quarry lake and should not be treated as representative of the shallow main lake.
- Porta Lake results are comparative and must remain separate from Massaciuccoli results.
- The study does not provide a predictive model linking the Digital Twin's land-cover or ecological-risk variables to future CH4 emissions.

Relevance to the Massaciuccoli Digital Twin:
- High value for retrieval questions about methane and CO2, dissolved oxygen, redox conditions, water-column stratification, organic-matter decomposition, reed beds, eutrophication and spatial heterogeneity of greenhouse-gas emissions.
- Useful for explaining why open water, reed margins and deep quarry waters can behave differently.
- Provides sample-specific quantitative reference values and clear examples of local emission hotspots.
- Does not directly calibrate the Random Forest ecological-risk emulator and does not provide a validated quantitative response function for future climate or land-use scenarios.
