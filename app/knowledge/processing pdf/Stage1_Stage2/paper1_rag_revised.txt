======================================================================
BACKGROUND
======================================================================

Wetlands are among the ecosystems most vulnerable to climate change.
Over the last century they have undergone a substantial reduction in
their global extent, resulting in biodiversity loss and degradation of
ecosystem services including water regulation, carbon sequestration,
food resources and tourism.

Climate change represents one of the principal drivers of wetland
degradation and biodiversity alteration. Predicting future ecological
conditions is therefore essential for supporting conservation,
environmental planning and ecosystem management.

======================================================================
ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE FOR BIODIVERSITY FORECASTING
======================================================================

Artificial Intelligence models can identify large-scale biodiversity
patterns and forecast future ecological changes before they become
observable.

Although environmental datasets naturally contain uncertainty,
machine-learning approaches are effective for detecting macroscopic
ecological trends that support long-term environmental decision making.

These models are intended to describe ecosystem-scale dynamics rather
than precise predictions for individual species occurrences.

======================================================================
MASSACIUCCOLI CASE STUDY
======================================================================

The study focuses on the Massaciuccoli Lake basin in Tuscany, Italy.

The basin represents a wetland of high ecological value characterized
by rich biodiversity and by the coexistence of native and non-native
species.

The ecosystem is exposed to multiple environmental pressures,
including climate change and land-use change, which influence habitat
suitability and future biodiversity patterns.

======================================================================
STUDY OBJECTIVES
======================================================================

The objective of the study is to evaluate how climate change may affect
future animal distributions within the Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

Machine-learning models are used to estimate habitat suitability under
different greenhouse-gas emission scenarios.

The resulting spatial predictions provide information that can support
environmental management, biodiversity monitoring and spatial planning.

======================================================================
INTRODUCTION
======================================================================

WETLAND ECOSYSTEMS

Wetlands are among the ecosystems most vulnerable to climate change.
During the twentieth century their global extent declined by
approximately 70%, with consequent reductions in biodiversity and in
ecosystem services including water quality, food production, carbon
sequestration and socio-economic functions.

Climate change is recognized as one of the principal drivers affecting
wetland ecosystems and biodiversity conservation. Long-term monitoring
is therefore essential for assessing ecosystem conditions and supporting
conservation activities.

======================================================================
ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE FOR BIODIVERSITY ANALYSIS
======================================================================

Artificial Intelligence and big-data mining approaches can identify
large-scale biodiversity patterns and forecast future ecological
changes before they become observable.

Although environmental datasets contain uncertainty and
misclassification, machine-learning models remain effective for
identifying macroscopic ecological trends over large spatial scales.

These models are intended to support ecosystem-scale assessments and
complement local ecological investigations rather than replace them.

======================================================================
MASSACIUCCOLI LAKE BASIN
======================================================================

The Massaciuccoli Lake basin, Tuscany (Italy), is a wetland ecosystem
characterized by high biodiversity and ecological complexity.

The basin hosts both native and non-native animal species and
represents an important migration area for birds.

Habitat suitability within the basin is influenced by hydrogeological,
chemical and environmental conditions.

Bird communities are particularly sensitive to alterations in
hydrological and biogeochemical conditions, making the basin suitable
for studying the ecological effects of climate change.

======================================================================
MODELLING OBJECTIVES
======================================================================

The modelling framework estimates the effects of climate change on
animal presence and species richness within the Massaciuccoli Lake
basin.

Future habitat suitability is projected under Representative
Concentration Pathway (RCP) 4.5 and RCP 8.5 scenarios for 2050 and
2100.

Historical analyses compare biodiversity conditions for 1950, 1999 and
2016.

Species richness is estimated through the combination of one
unsupervised and one supervised machine-learning model trained on 180
resident species.

Species occurrence information is obtained from GBIF and integrated
with validated field observations.

======================================================================
MAIN FINDINGS
======================================================================

Climate change is projected to substantially modify biodiversity within
the Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

Bird biodiversity is predicted to decline even under the medium
greenhouse-gas emission scenario.

Habitat suitability is projected to increase for several insect
species, while aquatic and terrestrial species are expected to modify
their spatial distribution toward cooler and wetter areas.

The predicted biodiversity changes are associated with potential
impacts on ecosystem services.

The modelling framework is based entirely on FAIR data, open datasets
and transferable methodology, allowing application to other wetland
ecosystems.

======================================================================
METHODS
======================================================================

OVERVIEW

The proposed methodology estimates long-term changes in animal species
richness within the Massaciuccoli Lake basin through a spatially explicit
machine-learning framework.

The workflow integrates environmental information, species occurrence
records and climate projections to estimate habitat suitability under
historical and future climate conditions.

The methodological pipeline consists of:

• Environmental data collection
• Environmental data re-analysis
• Species observation data collection
• Species prevalence estimation
• Machine-learning modelling
• Future scenario projection
• Spatial biodiversity assessment

======================================================================
CLIMATE SCENARIOS
======================================================================

Future environmental conditions are simulated through Representative
Concentration Pathway (RCP) scenarios.

The study considers both an intermediate-emission scenario (RCP 4.5)
and a high-emission scenario (RCP 8.5).

Species distributions are projected for 2050 and 2100 under both
climate scenarios and compared with historical environmental
conditions.

======================================================================
SPECIES PREVALENCE ESTIMATION
======================================================================

Species prevalence is estimated before ecological modelling in order to
reduce sampling imbalance among species.

The prevalence estimation procedure improves the representativeness of
species occurrence information and supports the construction of more
robust biodiversity models.

The estimated prevalence values are subsequently incorporated into the
machine-learning workflow.

======================================================================
MACHINE LEARNING FRAMEWORK
======================================================================

The proposed framework combines unsupervised and supervised
machine-learning approaches.

The unsupervised component estimates a spatial biodiversity indicator
based on species richness.

The supervised component learns the relationship between environmental
conditions and biodiversity patterns and projects these relationships to
historical and future climate scenarios.

The modelling framework produces spatially explicit biodiversity maps at
approximately 50 m spatial resolution across the entire
Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

======================================================================
MODEL OUTPUTS
======================================================================

The framework estimates changes in habitat suitability, species richness
and biodiversity distribution under historical and future environmental
conditions.

Outputs are generated for multiple temporal scenarios and allow direct
comparison between past, present and projected ecosystem conditions.

The resulting spatial predictions provide quantitative information that
supports biodiversity assessment, ecosystem monitoring and environmental
planning within the Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

======================================================================
ENVIRONMENTAL DATA
======================================================================

The environmental variables used in this study originate from open
datasets published by the District Basin Authority of the Northern
Apennines, the public authority responsible for hydrogeological
protection and water-resource management across Central and Northern
Italy.

The Authority provides openly accessible topographical,
hydrogeological, environmental and territorial information for the
Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

The environmental dataset describes the ecological conditions of the
basin through variables that directly influence habitat suitability and
species distribution.

The environmental variables include:

• Evapotranspiration (ET)

Amount of water transferred from the land surface to the atmosphere
through evaporation and plant transpiration.

• Water Surplus (WS)

Amount of precipitation exceeding evapotranspiration, representing the
availability of water resources within the basin.

• Climatic Water Deficit (C)

Difference between atmospheric water demand and available water,
indicating potential drought conditions affecting habitats.

• Land Use

Spatial distribution of natural and anthropogenic land-cover classes.

• Vegetation Characteristics

Environmental descriptors related to vegetation cover and habitat
structure.

These environmental layers constitute the predictor variables used by
the machine-learning models to estimate species richness and habitat
suitability throughout the Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

======================================================================
ENVIRONMENTAL DATA PREPROCESSING
======================================================================

Historical environmental observations are combined with climate-change
projections to reconstruct future environmental conditions across the
Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

Future scenarios are generated by modifying the environmental variables
according to Representative Concentration Pathway (RCP) projections.

The study considers both medium-emission (RCP 4.5) and high-emission
(RCP 8.5) scenarios and estimates future environmental conditions for
2050 and 2100.

All environmental datasets are spatially harmonised on a common grid,
allowing each grid cell to contain a complete and consistent set of
environmental descriptors suitable for machine-learning analysis.

======================================================================
SPECIES OBSERVATION DATA
======================================================================

Species occurrence information is collected from the Global
Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF), one of the largest public
repositories of biodiversity observations.

Public occurrence records are integrated with reliable field
observations collected directly within the Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

Quality-control procedures reduce inconsistencies and improve the
reliability of the occurrence dataset before ecological modelling.

The final dataset includes approximately 180 resident animal species
representing the biodiversity of the basin.

======================================================================
SPECIES PREVALENCE ASSESSMENT
======================================================================

Although citizen-science observations improved the overall quality of
the occurrence dataset, sub-sampling biases remained, primarily due to
the under-representation of rare species, which could lead
distribution models to underestimate their presence.

To reduce this bias, prior information regarding species presence and
commonness was incorporated into the modelling framework through
species prevalence.

Species prevalence was approximated as the prevalence of species in
Italian wetlands, assuming that species commonly observed in Italian
wetlands were also more likely to occur in the Massaciuccoli Lake
basin before analysing local observations.

Within the Bayesian modelling framework, these prior probabilities
were subsequently updated during model adaptation to the observed
data.

To estimate species commonness in Italian wetlands, public wetland
definitions from the Ramsar Convention were used together with GBIF
observations collected during the last decade.

Species observation distributions were analysed through an
unsupervised machine-learning approach to identify groups of species
sharing similar commonness characteristics.

Each identified cluster was associated with a prior prevalence value
ranging from 0.2 to 0.8, providing prior probabilities for the Bayesian
species-distribution models.

Species commonness was characterised through numerical descriptors
derived from the observation records, including species abundance,
observation frequency and habitat extension.

These descriptors were used by the clustering algorithm to identify
groups of species exhibiting similar occurrence patterns across Italian
wetlands.

======================================================================
MACHINE-LEARNING MODELLING
======================================================================

The modelling framework combines one unsupervised and one supervised
machine-learning model to estimate long-term changes in species
richness within the Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

The unsupervised model estimates species prevalence by analysing
species commonness patterns across Italian wetlands.

The supervised model combines species occurrence information,
environmental variables and prior prevalence estimates to predict
habitat suitability and species richness under historical and future
environmental conditions.

The modelling strategy follows a Bayesian formulation in which prior
probabilities describing species prevalence are combined with evidence
derived from environmental conditions and species occurrence records.

This approach reduces the influence of observation biases and allows
prior ecological knowledge to contribute to habitat suitability
estimation.

Environmental variables are associated with species occurrence through
Ecological Niche Modelling (ENM), allowing the estimation of habitat
suitability under different environmental scenarios.

The resulting habitat suitability maps constitute the basis for the
subsequent estimation of spatial species richness.

======================================================================
ECOLOGICAL NICHE MODELLING
======================================================================

Ecological Niche Models estimate the relationship between
environmental conditions and species occurrence.

The models infer the environmental niche of each species and project
habitat suitability under historical and future environmental
conditions.

The modelling process combines environmental predictor variables with
species occurrence records to estimate the probability that suitable
habitat exists at each spatial location.

======================================================================
HABITAT SUITABILITY
======================================================================

Habitat suitability is estimated independently for each species over
the entire study area.

The resulting suitability maps are subsequently combined to estimate
species richness and analyse biodiversity dynamics across the
Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

Spatial predictions are produced for historical conditions and for the
future climate scenarios considered in this study.

======================================================================
SPATIAL RESOLUTION
======================================================================

The modelling framework produces spatially explicit biodiversity maps
at approximately 50 m spatial resolution.

This spatial representation allows biodiversity changes to be analysed
at the ecosystem scale while preserving local spatial variability.

Spatially explicit predictions complement traditional basin-wide
biodiversity indicators and provide information useful for ecosystem
assessment and spatial planning.

======================================================================
ECOLOGICAL NICHE MODELLING
======================================================================

Ecological Niche Models (ENMs) estimate the set of environmental
resources and conditions underlying the suitability of an area for a
species, representing its fundamental ecological niche.

An ENM estimates habitat suitability as the set of locations whose
environmental conditions fall within the ecological niche of the
species.

Within the native distribution area, an ENM estimates the realised
species distribution. Outside the native range, the model estimates the
potential distribution, identifying environmentally suitable habitats.
Mathematically, a species' ecological niche is represented as a
hypervolume in a multidimensional environmental space, where each
dimension corresponds to one environmental variable.

The objective of the model is to identify the environmental
hypervolume associated with the persistence of the species.

The capability of an ENM to correctly estimate habitat suitability
depends on the availability of a sufficiently informative set of
environmental predictors describing the ecological requirements of the
species.
Ecological Niche Models can be implemented through statistical
methods, machine-learning algorithms or expert-defined ecological
rules.

In this study, ENMs are used to estimate habitat suitability for each
species under historical and future environmental conditions within
the Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

======================================================================
MAXENT MODELLING
======================================================================

The ecological niche models are implemented through MaxEnt, a
presence-only machine-learning algorithm that estimates the
probability distribution of a species under the principle of maximum
entropy.

MaxEnt estimates habitat suitability by relating observed species
occurrences to environmental variables while constraining the model
with the environmental characteristics of the observation sites.

The resulting suitability maps represent the probability that each
location provides suitable environmental conditions for the analysed
species.

======================================================================
MODEL TRAINING
======================================================================

Species occurrence records collected during the period 2013–2023 are
used for model training.

Training is performed using the environmental conditions associated
with the same temporal period.

Temperature and precipitation data from 2016 are used together with
the resampled environmental variables representing evapotranspiration,
water surplus and conductivity.

The models focus on long-term habitat suitability trends rather than on
naturally oscillating population densities.

======================================================================
HABITAT THRESHOLDING
======================================================================

For each species, MaxEnt provides a suitability probability
distribution.

Habitat suitability is transformed into a binary suitable/unsuitable
classification through a decision threshold estimated during model
training.

The selected threshold corresponds to the minimum suitability value
that correctly classifies all training presence locations.

The same threshold is subsequently applied to all historical and future
projection scenarios, ensuring methodological consistency across
temporal comparisons.

======================================================================
MODEL ROBUSTNESS
======================================================================

Although individual species models may retain uncertainty,
particularly for rarely observed species, the overall biodiversity
assessment relies on the statistical aggregation of a large number of
independent ecological niche models.

The combination of multiple species models produces more stable and
reliable macroscopic ecological information than the interpretation of
individual species predictions alone.

The objective of the framework is to estimate long-term biodiversity
patterns and their temporal evolution rather than precise predictions
for single species occurrences.

======================================================================
NOVELTY OF THE MODELLING APPROACH
======================================================================

The proposed framework combines Ecological Niche Modelling and
machine-learning techniques to estimate long-term biodiversity changes
within the Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

Unlike traditional Species Distribution Models, the framework estimates
species richness directly from the aggregation of habitat suitability
predictions generated for multiple species.

The modelling strategy combines prior ecological knowledge,
environmental predictors and species occurrence observations within a
Bayesian framework, reducing the influence of observation bias while
preserving ecological consistency.

The integration of unsupervised and supervised machine-learning models
allows biodiversity patterns to be reconstructed under both historical
and future climate scenarios.

The proposed methodology is entirely based on FAIR environmental data
and publicly available biodiversity observations, making the modelling
framework reproducible and transferable to other wetland ecosystems.

======================================================================
SPECIES RICHNESS ESTIMATION
======================================================================

Species richness is estimated by combining the habitat suitability
predictions generated independently for each analysed species.

For every spatial cell, the framework evaluates habitat suitability for
all modelled species and estimates biodiversity as the number of species
whose predicted habitat is suitable.

This approach produces spatially explicit biodiversity maps describing
the distribution of species richness across the Massaciuccoli Lake
basin.

Species richness is estimated consistently for historical observations
and future climate scenarios, allowing direct temporal comparison of
biodiversity dynamics.

======================================================================
APPLICATION TO THE MASSACIUCCOLI LAKE BASIN
======================================================================

The modelling framework is applied to the entire Massaciuccoli Lake
basin using harmonised environmental variables and species occurrence
records.

Environmental predictors are available for each spatial cell, allowing
the reconstruction of biodiversity patterns over the complete study
area.

Historical biodiversity conditions are compared with future projections
generated under Representative Concentration Pathway (RCP) 4.5 and
RCP 8.5 climate scenarios.

The resulting biodiversity maps support ecosystem monitoring,
conservation planning and the assessment of climate-change impacts
within the basin.

======================================================================
TEMPORAL SCENARIOS
======================================================================

The study evaluates biodiversity under multiple temporal conditions.

Historical analyses describe ecosystem conditions for past decades,
whereas future projections estimate biodiversity for 2050 and 2100
under medium- and high-emission climate scenarios.

The consistent application of the same modelling framework across all
temporal scenarios allows changes in habitat suitability and species
richness to be directly compared through time.

======================================================================
RESULTS
======================================================================

The modelling framework reconstructs historical biodiversity patterns
and projects future changes in species richness and habitat suitability
within the Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

Historical simulations provide a coherent reconstruction of biodiversity
distribution across the basin and constitute the reference conditions
for future climate projections.

Future biodiversity is evaluated under Representative Concentration
Pathway (RCP) 4.5 and RCP 8.5 climate scenarios for the years 2050 and
2100.

======================================================================
HISTORICAL BIODIVERSITY DISTRIBUTION
======================================================================

Historical simulations indicate that the modelling framework is able to
reconstruct coherent biodiversity patterns throughout the
Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

Areas characterised by favourable environmental conditions exhibit
higher predicted species richness, whereas environmentally stressed
areas show lower habitat suitability and reduced biodiversity.

The reconstructed historical biodiversity maps provide the ecological
baseline against which future climate scenarios are compared.

======================================================================
FUTURE CLIMATE SCENARIOS
======================================================================

Future climate projections indicate substantial modifications in habitat
suitability and biodiversity distribution throughout the
Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

The magnitude of the predicted changes depends on the greenhouse-gas
emission scenario considered, with stronger alterations generally
associated with the high-emission scenario.

Projected biodiversity changes become progressively more pronounced
towards the end of the century.

======================================================================
BIRD BIODIVERSITY
======================================================================

Bird species exhibit the largest overall reduction in habitat
suitability among the analysed taxonomic groups.

The predicted decline is already evident under the medium-emission
scenario and becomes more pronounced under the high-emission scenario.

The simulations indicate that bird communities are particularly
sensitive to long-term climatic changes affecting the
Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

======================================================================
AQUATIC AND TERRESTRIAL SPECIES
======================================================================

The projected response to climate change differs among taxonomic
groups.

Several aquatic and terrestrial species tend to shift their potential
distribution toward cooler and more water-rich areas.

The simulations indicate that future biodiversity patterns will be
characterised by spatial redistribution rather than uniform species
loss.

======================================================================
INSECT SPECIES
======================================================================

Unlike bird communities, several insect species are projected to
experience an increase in habitat suitability under future climate
conditions.

The predicted increase reflects differences in ecological requirements
among taxonomic groups and highlights the heterogeneous response of
biodiversity to climate change.

======================================================================
SPATIAL BIODIVERSITY DYNAMICS
======================================================================

The modelling framework reveals spatially heterogeneous biodiversity
responses across the Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

Different portions of the basin exhibit distinct temporal trajectories,
reflecting local environmental conditions and their interaction with
future climate scenarios.

Spatial biodiversity maps provide detailed information on the location,
magnitude and distribution of projected ecological changes.

======================================================================
MODEL VALIDATION
======================================================================

The proposed modelling framework was evaluated by comparing predicted
species distributions with independent occurrence observations.

Model performance was assessed separately for each analysed species,
allowing the reliability of habitat suitability predictions to be
quantified across the study area.

The evaluation indicates that the modelling framework is capable of
reconstructing realistic biodiversity patterns within the
Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

======================================================================
MODEL PERFORMANCE
======================================================================

Prediction accuracy varies among species according to the amount and
quality of the available occurrence data.

Species characterised by abundant and well-distributed observations
generally exhibit higher predictive performance, whereas species with
limited observations retain greater uncertainty.

Despite this variability, the aggregation of multiple species models
produces stable biodiversity estimates at the ecosystem scale.

======================================================================
MODEL LIMITATIONS
======================================================================

Prediction uncertainty is primarily associated with incomplete species
observations and with the limited availability of occurrence data for
rare species.

Additional uncertainty derives from future climate projections and from
the assumption that species maintain comparable ecological niches under
future environmental conditions.

These limitations should be considered when interpreting projected
changes in habitat suitability and biodiversity distribution.

======================================================================
ECOSYSTEM-SCALE INTERPRETATION
======================================================================

Although uncertainty may affect individual species predictions, the
framework is designed to estimate macroscopic biodiversity dynamics
rather than precise forecasts for single species.

The aggregation of habitat suitability predictions across a large
number of resident species provides robust information regarding
long-term ecosystem evolution.

The modelling framework therefore supports biodiversity assessment at
the ecosystem scale rather than species-specific ecological forecasting.

======================================================================
CONCLUSIONS
======================================================================

The proposed hybrid Digital Twin framework combines machine learning,
Ecological Niche Modelling and environmental data to estimate long-term
changes in biodiversity within the Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

The framework reconstructs historical biodiversity patterns and projects
future ecosystem conditions under multiple climate scenarios.

The results indicate that climate change is expected to substantially
modify biodiversity distribution, with different responses among
taxonomic groups and increasing impacts under higher greenhouse-gas
emission scenarios.
The methodology relies exclusively on FAIR environmental datasets and
public biodiversity observations, making the proposed framework
reproducible and transferable to similar wetland ecosystems.

The resulting spatial biodiversity maps provide quantitative support for
ecosystem monitoring, environmental assessment and long-term planning.
The hybrid Digital Twin demonstrates how machine-learning techniques
can be integrated with ecological modelling to support biodiversity
assessment under changing environmental conditions while preserving a
transparent and reproducible scientific workflow.

======================================================================
RESULT SUMMARY
======================================================================

The analyses indicate a continuous increase in temperature from the
historical period towards all future climate scenarios.

Projected precipitation patterns suggest an increasing concentration of
rainfall in mountain areas together with progressively drier conditions
across most of the Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

The combined effect of these environmental changes is expected to
produce substantial modifications in biodiversity distribution,
particularly after 2050.

Bird communities are projected to undergo the strongest habitat loss,
whereas several insect species are expected to increase their habitat
suitability.

Other terrestrial and aquatic species are predicted to progressively
shift towards cooler and more water-rich areas.

These projected biodiversity changes are consistent with the overall
environmental differences observed between historical and future
climate scenarios.

======================================================================
IMPLICATIONS FOR ECOSYSTEM MANAGEMENT
======================================================================

The projected biodiversity changes indicate increasing risks for the
ecological integrity of the Massaciuccoli Lake basin and for the
ecosystem services depending on resident species.

The predicted timing and magnitude of biodiversity change suggest that
mitigation and adaptation measures should be implemented before the
most severe ecological transitions occur.

Water management, ecosystem restoration and biodiversity conservation
strategies may reduce part of the projected impacts if implemented
sufficiently early.

The quantitative estimates produced by the modelling framework provide
scientific support for long-term environmental planning and ecosystem
management.

======================================================================
ADVANTAGES OF THE FRAMEWORK
======================================================================

The proposed framework combines FAIR environmental datasets,
Ecological Niche Modelling and machine-learning techniques within a
fully reproducible workflow.

The methodology is transferable to other wetland ecosystems because it
does not depend on proprietary datasets or location-specific software.

Spatially explicit biodiversity maps complement traditional aggregated
biodiversity indicators by providing detailed information on the
location and intensity of ecological change.

======================================================================
MODEL LIMITATIONS
======================================================================

Species distribution models inevitably simplify ecological processes.

The framework estimates habitat suitability rather than actual species
abundance and does not explicitly model ecological interactions among
species.

Future projections also depend on the uncertainty associated with
climate scenarios and environmental assumptions.

For these reasons, the framework should be interpreted as a tool for
ecosystem-scale biodiversity assessment rather than precise prediction
of individual species occurrence.

======================================================================
OVERALL CONCLUSIONS
======================================================================

The proposed methodology demonstrates how Artificial Intelligence,
Ecological Niche Modelling and FAIR environmental data can be combined
to support biodiversity assessment within a Digital Twin framework.

The resulting biodiversity projections provide quantitative knowledge
for ecosystem monitoring, conservation planning and climate-change
adaptation.

The methodology remains reproducible, transparent and transferable,
making it suitable for long-term environmental assessment in
Massaciuccoli Lake and in other wetland ecosystems.

======================================================================
MODEL PREDICTION BEYOND THE TRAINING RANGES
======================================================================

The ecological niche models were trained directly on environmental
conditions observed within the Massaciuccoli Lake basin rather than on
global-scale environmental datasets.

Training on regional environmental conditions allows the models to
capture ecological gradients that are relevant at the basin scale and
to better represent local habitat suitability.

Large-scale environmental datasets may improve the estimation of
species tolerance ranges but can reduce sensitivity to local
environmental variability.

The adopted approach combines local environmental information with
prior ecological knowledge, improving regional habitat prediction while
reducing the influence of sampling bias.

======================================================================

======================================================================

The framework is designed to estimate long-term biodiversity dynamics
rather than exact species presence at individual locations.

Model aggregation across many species increases the robustness of
ecosystem-scale predictions and reduces the influence of uncertainty
associated with individual ecological niche models.

The resulting biodiversity maps should therefore be interpreted as
macroscopic ecological indicators rather than deterministic forecasts.
