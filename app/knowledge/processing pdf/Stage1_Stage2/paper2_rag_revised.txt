ABSTRACT
======================================================================

Wetlands are among the ecosystems most exposed to degradation due to
the combined action of anthropogenic pressures and climate-related
stressors, particularly in highly anthropised landscapes.

This study presents a multi-model methodology for assessing and mapping
the risk of forest and grassland habitat degradation together with the
associated biodiversity loss in wetland ecosystems.

The framework integrates three complementary unsupervised approaches:

• deterministic cumulative risk assessment;

• multivariate clustering analysis;

• deep-learning risk assessment based on a Variational Autoencoder
  combined with cluster analysis.

The methodology is applied to the Massaciuccoli Lake basin, a
Ramsar-listed wetland characterised by high ecological value and strong
land-use pressure.

Risk assessment is based on twelve environmental stressor variables
describing ecological fragility and harmful driving forces at
approximately 50 m spatial resolution.

The three models generate spatially explicit ecosystem-risk maps that
identify complementary hotspot distributions.

Areas consistently classified as medium or high risk across multiple
models represent robust ecosystem-risk hotspots, whereas divergent
classifications indicate transitional or uncertain ecological
conditions requiring further investigation.

The proposed methodology is reproducible, scalable and transferable to
other wetland ecosystems, providing scientific support for conservation
planning, environmental management and spatial decision-making.

======================================================================
INTRODUCTION
======================================================================

Wetlands are among the most productive ecosystems on Earth and provide
essential ecosystem services including biodiversity conservation, water
purification, flood regulation, carbon storage and climate regulation.

Despite their ecological importance, wetlands are increasingly exposed
to multiple anthropogenic pressures, including land-use change,
urbanisation, agricultural intensification, habitat fragmentation and
climate change.

The simultaneous action of multiple stressors produces cumulative
ecological effects that are often difficult to quantify using
traditional environmental assessment methods.

The complexity of wetland ecosystems therefore requires integrated
approaches capable of combining heterogeneous environmental
information into coherent ecosystem-risk indicators.

======================================================================
ECOSYSTEM RISK ASSESSMENT
======================================================================

Ecosystem Risk Assessment (ERA) aims to identify, quantify and map the
probability that ecological systems experience degradation due to
natural or anthropogenic stressors.

Risk is generally interpreted as the combination of ecosystem exposure,
ecological vulnerability and the intensity of environmental stressors.

Spatial ecosystem-risk assessment supports environmental monitoring,
conservation planning and prioritisation of management actions by
identifying areas characterised by different ecological risk levels.

======================================================================
LIMITATIONS OF TRADITIONAL APPROACHES
======================================================================

Traditional ecosystem-risk assessment methods frequently rely on
deterministic models or on individual environmental indicators that may
be insufficient to describe the complexity of ecological systems.

Single-model approaches may fail to capture complementary aspects of
ecosystem behaviour, particularly when environmental variables exhibit
non-linear relationships or complex interactions.

The integration of multiple modelling approaches may therefore provide
a more comprehensive representation of ecosystem risk.

======================================================================
MULTI-MODEL RISK ASSESSMENT
======================================================================

Different modelling approaches describe different characteristics of
ecosystem behaviour.

Deterministic cumulative-risk models quantify the combined effect of
multiple environmental stressors.

Clustering techniques identify groups of areas characterised by similar
ecological conditions.

Deep-learning models detect complex non-linear environmental patterns
that may not be identified through traditional statistical methods.

The integration of multiple independent models allows complementary
sources of ecological information to contribute to ecosystem-risk
assessment.

MASSACIUCCOLI LAKE BASIN
======================================================================

The Massaciuccoli Lake basin represents a complex wetland ecosystem
characterised by high biodiversity, heterogeneous environmental
conditions and multiple anthropogenic pressures.

The coexistence of natural habitats, agricultural areas and urbanised
zones makes the basin particularly suitable for evaluating integrated
ecosystem-risk assessment methodologies.

Its ecological relevance and the availability of detailed environmental
datasets make the basin an appropriate case study for developing and
evaluating reproducible ecosystem-risk assessment frameworks.

======================================================================
OBJECTIVES OF THE STUDY
======================================================================

This study proposes a multi-model ecosystem-risk assessment framework
that combines deterministic cumulative-risk analysis, clustering
techniques and deep-learning models.

The objective is to produce spatially explicit ecosystem-risk maps able
to identify robust ecological hotspots and to characterise areas
affected by different levels of environmental risk.

The proposed methodology is evaluated through its application to the
Massaciuccoli Lake basin and is designed to be reproducible and
transferable to other wetland ecosystems.

======================================================================
ECOSYSTEM RISK ASSESSMENT (ERA)
======================================================================

Ecosystem Risk Assessment (ERA) is a methodological framework for
identifying, characterising and spatially representing the likelihood
that an ecosystem undergoes a critical transition from one ecological
state to another due to natural or anthropogenic pressures.

Unlike traditional impact assessments focused on individual stressors,
ERA explicitly considers the simultaneous action of multiple pressure
factors, their interactions across different spatial and temporal
scales, and the non-linear responses of ecological systems.

In wetland ecosystems, ERA is particularly relevant because ecological
dynamics emerge from the interaction among hydrology, vegetation,
biodiversity, climate variability and land-use practices. These same
interactions also increase ecosystem vulnerability to cumulative
anthropogenic pressures and irreversible ecological transitions.

======================================================================
DETERMINISTIC RISK ASSESSMENT
======================================================================

Deterministic ecosystem-risk assessment methods are among the most
widely adopted approaches because of their transparency,
interpretability and ease of communication for environmental planning
and policy support.

These methodologies generally rely on cumulative impact maps,
multi-criteria decision analysis and the spatial aggregation of
standardised environmental stressors.

Although robust and easily interpretable, deterministic approaches
often require subjective weighting schemes, assume simplified
relationships among stressors and may have limited transferability
across heterogeneous ecological contexts.

======================================================================
STATISTICAL AND MULTIVARIATE APPROACHES
======================================================================

Statistical and multivariate methodologies aim to identify recurring
ecological conditions and emergent environmental gradients directly
from observational data.

Clustering techniques and latent-variable analyses reduce part of the
subjectivity associated with deterministic methods by identifying
co-occurrence patterns among environmental stressors.

However, these approaches are generally descriptive and may have
limited capability to represent highly non-linear ecological
relationships.

======================================================================
MACHINE LEARNING AND DEEP LEARNING
======================================================================

The increasing availability of harmonised geospatial datasets has
favoured the adoption of machine-learning and deep-learning techniques
for ecosystem risk assessment.

Unsupervised approaches, including advanced clustering, anomaly
detection and deep generative models based on autoencoders, enable the
identification of latent ecological structures, emergent gradients and
early-warning signals associated with ecosystem change.

These approaches improve the representation of non-linear ecological
relationships and facilitate the integration of heterogeneous
environmental variables.

Nevertheless, single-model approaches still present limitations in
terms of ecological interpretability, result comparability and overall
assessment robustness.

======================================================================
MULTI-MODEL ECOSYSTEM RISK ASSESSMENT
======================================================================

Recent studies increasingly support integrated ecosystem-risk
assessment frameworks that combine deterministic, structural and
data-driven methodologies within a common analytical architecture.

The complementary nature of these approaches improves the reliability,
interpretability and robustness of ecosystem-risk assessments while
reducing methodological bias associated with any individual modelling
strategy.

======================================================================
PREVIOUS STUDIES ON THE MASSACIUCCOLI LAKE BASIN
======================================================================

Previous ecosystem studies on the Massaciuccoli Lake basin have mainly
focused on specific environmental components or restricted portions of
the territory.

These investigations include:

• hydrological dynamics and water quality;

• anthropogenic pressures associated with agriculture, urbanisation
  and habitat fragmentation;

• habitats of community interest and vegetation dynamics;

• biodiversity conservation;

• ecological effects of climate change.

Despite the richness of these thematic investigations, no previous
study has provided a comprehensive, spatially explicit ecosystem-risk
assessment integrating anthropogenic pressures, climatic drivers,
ecological fragility and biodiversity indicators across the entire
Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

======================================================================
NOVELTY OF THE PROPOSED FRAMEWORK
======================================================================

The proposed methodology represents the first multi-model comparative
ecosystem-risk assessment conducted for the entire Massaciuccoli Lake
basin.

Its main novelty lies in the explicit integration and systematic
comparison of three complementary analytical paradigms:

• deterministic cumulative assessment;

• structural analysis based on clustering;

• predictive analysis based on a Variational Autoencoder integrated
  with cluster analysis.

Rather than identifying a single optimal model, the methodology
analyses agreements and disagreements among complementary models to
produce a more robust hierarchical representation of ecosystem risk.

Areas consistently classified as high risk by multiple models identify
priority ecosystem-risk hotspots, whereas divergent classifications
highlight transitional or uncertain ecological conditions requiring
further investigation.

The resulting framework is reproducible, scalable and transferable to
other wetland ecosystems characterised by heterogeneous environmental
stressors and harmonised geospatial datasets.

METHODOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK
======================================================================

The ecosystem risk assessment methodology was designed as a
multi-phase analytical framework integrating deterministic,
clustering, statistical and deep-learning approaches.

The workflow was developed to maximise analytical robustness,
transparency and reproducibility while maintaining ecological
interpretability.

The methodology consists of three main phases:

• spatial and temporal characterisation of the study area;

• ecosystem risk modelling through complementary analytical models;

• interpretation and comparison of the resulting ecosystem-risk
  assessments.

DETERMINISTIC ECOSYSTEM RISK MODEL
======================================================================

The deterministic model estimates ecosystem risk through the cumulative
combination of multiple environmental stressors describing ecological
fragility and harmful driving forces acting within the
Massaciuccoli Lake basin.

Each environmental variable is normalised to a common scale before
being combined into a cumulative ecosystem-risk indicator.

The deterministic assessment provides a transparent and easily
interpretable representation of ecosystem risk, facilitating ecological
interpretation and environmental decision-making.

The resulting risk map represents the spatial distribution of
cumulative environmental pressure throughout the study area.

======================================================================
CLUSTERING-BASED RISK ASSESSMENT
======================================================================

The clustering approach identifies groups of spatial cells sharing
similar environmental characteristics without requiring predefined
ecosystem-risk classes.

Environmental similarity is evaluated simultaneously across all
available environmental variables, allowing natural ecological
structures to emerge directly from the data.

The resulting clusters describe areas characterised by comparable
environmental conditions and provide an alternative representation of
ecosystem-risk patterns independent of deterministic assumptions.

======================================================================
DEEP-LEARNING RISK ASSESSMENT
======================================================================

The third analytical component is based on a Variational Autoencoder
(VAE), an unsupervised deep-learning architecture designed to identify
latent environmental structures within high-dimensional datasets.

The VAE learns a compressed representation of the environmental
conditions characterising each spatial location while preserving the
most informative ecological patterns.

Latent representations generated by the VAE are subsequently analysed
through clustering techniques in order to identify ecosystem-risk
classes associated with similar environmental behaviour.

This approach enables the detection of complex non-linear ecological
relationships that may not be captured by deterministic or traditional
statistical methods.

======================================================================
LATENT REPRESENTATION
======================================================================

The latent space generated by the Variational Autoencoder provides a
compact representation of the environmental state of each spatial cell.

Neighbouring points in the latent space correspond to spatial locations
sharing similar ecological characteristics, even when these
relationships are difficult to identify within the original
high-dimensional environmental space.

The latent representation therefore facilitates the identification of
hidden ecological gradients and transitional ecosystem conditions.

======================================================================
MODEL COMPARISON
======================================================================

Rather than selecting a single preferred methodology, the proposed
framework compares the results generated by three complementary
ecosystem-risk assessment approaches.

Agreements among independent models increase confidence in the
identified ecosystem-risk patterns.

Conversely, disagreements highlight environmentally transitional or
uncertain areas that may require additional ecological investigation.

The comparison therefore provides information not only on ecosystem
risk itself, but also on the robustness of the assessment.

ECOSYSTEM RISK HOTSPOTS
======================================================================

Areas consistently classified as medium or high risk by multiple
independent models are interpreted as robust ecosystem-risk hotspots.

These areas represent locations where different analytical approaches
independently identify elevated ecological vulnerability.

Conversely, areas receiving inconsistent classifications indicate
transitional environmental conditions or higher modelling uncertainty.

The joint interpretation of multiple models therefore provides a more
robust ecosystem-risk assessment than any individual modelling
approach considered separately.
